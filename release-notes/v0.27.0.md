# Through the looking glass

People step into the Reflection: they read, write and discuss the pages their agent keeps, and the agent works right beside them.

## New & Noteworthy

**The Reflection in Fluid.** In 0.26.0 the agent learned to write pages for people. But people could only reach them through their agent, the files on disk or the REST API. Now they get a real place for them. Inside a domain with a Reflection, the top bar has a switch: Engrams or Reflection.

- The sidebar shows the pages by title, with the open threads per page, plus How we write (the pact) and Review with a count of open threads.
- The Reflection home starts with a large search field. Below it are the pact, a Review card that says what waits ("3 pages need an update."), the open threads by page and the list of all pages.
- A page shows its sections. Each section heading has a small "N sources" count with the engrams it was built from. A dot marks it when one of them changed, was retired or is gone. A reader without engram rights never sees them.
- Somebody is editing? The page says so ("ada editing now").

**Comments on selected text.** Select a passage, press Comment, write. The thread sits on that exact text and the page highlights it. Answer, resolve and reopen right in the threads column. On a phone the threads open as a sheet. When the text changes, the thread says so. When the section is gone, the thread moves to "Not on the page any more". Nothing gets lost.

**Live editing.** Edit opens the page source in the same live editor engrams have. Several people (and agents) type in one page at once. The hidden section ids stay hidden, and Enter, Backspace or Delete cannot break them by accident. Retype a heading and its threads stay with it. When a save fails or the connection drops, the tab keeps your text as a draft and offers it back next time. It keeps the last three, newest first, each with a line of its text. Put one back and the others stay on offer.

**Pages and files.** Create, move and delete pages from Fluid. Write the pact when there is none yet. Paste, drop or attach images and files in the editor. A page lists its files. Curators replace, move and remove them, and a move rewrites every link. Two people uploading the same name at the same moment both keep their file: the second one gets the next free name.

**The review screen.** One place for what waits: the open threads (split into "Waiting for people" and "Waiting for the agent") and below them the reflect queue with missing and unused files. Everything is grouped by page, and a diff shows only the changed lines with a little context.

**A new look for Fluid.** Fluid got calmer. The domain and the Engrams / Reflection switch sit in the top bar, next to the logo. Theme, text size, width, keyboard shortcuts, the handbook and Maintenance moved into one menu behind your avatar. The sidebar is simpler: Overview first, then the tree with a count per folder. Reading pages have one "More actions" menu. The details rail is quieter and shows "Linked from" by title. Threads have no boxes any more. Dark mode is a neutral dark now, not blue. On a phone the sidebar is a drawer and the menu is a sheet.

- A domain opens on its MANIFEST, as a page to read: the lead, When to Use, Scope and the other sections in file order. The configuration sections sit once in a rail beside it, with what was recorded here lately. The engrams follow as rows.
- The rare settings have a screen of their own (`/d/<domain>/settings`): members, review mode, domain policies, provisioning, tag aliases, kept texts, backup and the danger zone.

**Editing in the reading column.** Both editors now stand in the same column as the page they edit. The format bar stays in view while you scroll. The header shows who is in the page (avatars in their own colors), one save state and Done. The cursor of every other person carries their name.

- The engram's frontmatter is a form in a Details rail beside the text: Type, Status, Tags, a new Description field, Valid from and to, Salience. Raw shows it in the text.
- Done is the one way out. With unsaved text it asks "Leave the editor?" first. In a live page it asks for a save and waits for it. If the save does not land, your text is kept as a draft and the editor says so.
- Left the editor while an upload was still running? When it fails, or the file lands but its link was not added, a notice says so. It names the file and the page and stays until you dismiss it.
- The engram Move dialog lists the links it could not rewrite.

**Print.** Print an engram or a Reflection page and it looks like a printout, not like a web page on paper. The first page has a title block, with a small QR code of the page's web address at the top right. Links to other sites print their address, and menus and buttons stay off paper. It is always light, also from dark mode. In Chrome a running header and footer carry the domain, the title, the address, the print date and the page numbers. Safari and Firefox don't draw them, so there the title block carries the address and the date. Open threads of a Reflection page print as numbered notes at the end, and an engram ends with a short Related section (Linked from and its attachments). Reflection pages have Print view in their More actions menu too.

**Kept texts.** When a page or engram that people edit live cannot be saved (a conflict, a refused save, the Reflection switched off under it), the server keeps the text in `kept/` in its state folder. Now you can find it again.

- An editor tells you when the server kept unsaved text for its page and lets you put it back or drop it. In the plain editor the kept copy goes with your next save after you put it back. In a live page you drop it yourself once it is saved. Domain settings list the kept texts you may see. A Reflection reader finds their own kept comment beside the threads of the page.
- `crystalline kept list|show|drop` handles them. `drop` asks first, and needs `--yes` without a terminal or with `--json`.
- `crystalline status` shows `Kept texts: N (crystalline kept list)` while any are kept. `crystalline doctor` lists them and warns about ones older than 30 days. `doctor --fix` never deletes them.
- The REST API has `GET /api/v1/domains/{domain}/kept`, `GET .../kept/{id}` and `DELETE .../kept/{id}`. A text goes only to a person who may write its source. A draft's text goes only to its owner. A kept comment goes to the person who sent it and to the people who edit the Reflection. Each entry names the account that sent it (`sender_account`).
- A renamed domain takes its texts along. A removed domain's texts move to `kept/.removed/` and are never deleted, so a new domain with the same name never sees them.

**The Reflection on a shared database.** Several instances can share one Postgres database. Until now every one of them served a domain's Reflection from its own disk. Now the instance that hosts the domain serves it, and the others answer with its name (409).

- An instance is named by its address (`service.public_url`) or its machine's hostname, not by its id. Two with the same name get the start of their id, like `build-box (3f2a)`. On an instance where agents sign in (`auth.mcp`), people who are not signed in see "another instance".
- A host that crashed or went to sleep is taken over after 90 seconds without a heartbeat. The next write does it (a page write, a comment, an upload, opening the editor) or the next sync. Reading never moves it, and a signed-in reader is told when the host was last seen. Keep the clocks of all instances in sync.
- A write that was already on its way when its instance lost the host (a laptop that went to sleep) writes nothing to the shared index. It gets the same answer, its text is kept (`crystalline kept list`) and the files it wrote here are put back.
- After a host move, engrams, pages and files that exist only on the old host are reported, never deleted. A write, edit, move or delete onto such a path is refused (409) instead of replacing the only copy. Copy the folder over and sync, or run `crystalline sync --domain <name> --accept-disk`.

**Files of a mounted domain.** An agent that reads a domain served by another machine now gets its images and files too, for engrams and for the Reflection. The other server needs 0.27.0. Rights are checked there, the same as for a local read.

## Fixes

- In the reflect queue, a Reflection reader without engram rights saw the file names of engram attachments that a page links. They see the row now, but not the names.
- On a shared database, an instance that did not host a file domain could still write its engrams and attachments. The host's next walk then dropped the row without a word, and the text stayed only on the writer's disk. Such a write is refused now and names the host (409). A host that went silent for 90 seconds is taken over by the write instead. A tag rename or merge over all domains leaves such a domain out and names it.
- Moving an engram did not lock the files it wrote. An edit that landed at the same moment, to the moved engram or to an engram that links to it, could be lost or bring the old file back as a second engram. Two moves at once could also leave a link to an old address. Moves now lock their files and run one at a time, and a link a move could not rewrite is named in its answer (`links_failed`).
- An engram open in the editor and moved to another folder at the moment the editor saved could show as deleted, with a conflict. The save lands in the new folder now.
- The engram editor said "This engram was deleted" whenever its live session ended, also when it was not deleted at all (for example when a share link to a draft was taken back). Now it says the session ended and that your text is kept as a draft.
- An agent edit to an engram open in the live editor could vanish when that editor was in a conflict or its save had failed. The edit now goes to the file and the answer says so (`landed: file` with a note for the person).
- A semantic search answered nothing when the domains it searched had no vectors yet but other domains had. This hit a search that names domains and a search by somebody who cannot see every domain. It searches by words now, as Reflection search already did.
- When the Reflection was switched on for a folder that already held pages in `reflections/`, pages indexed as engrams stayed engrams. The Reflection showed no pages, or only some, until the next restart or full sync. This hit a switch in the domain policies, in the MANIFEST editor, in a plain editor or through an agent. It also hit moving or deleting the last engram out of `reflections/`. All pages show up at once now. Switching it off turns them back into engrams at once as well.
- On macOS and Windows, moving an engram or a Reflection file to a name that differs only in letter case was refused ("already exists"). Moving a Reflection page that way hung and blocked the page until a restart. All three work now.
- A Reflection file move through the REST API could change the extension (`flow.png` to `flow.jpg`), and the file was then served with the wrong type. A move keeps the extension now.
- In a domain that reviews changes, editing an engram whose file was missing on your disk made a draft without its frontmatter (title, type, tags, permalink). Now the edit says the file is missing and asks you to sync or restore it first.
- A daemon that took a domain over with `crystalline sync --take-over` did not watch its files until the next restart. It does at once now.
- In the engram editor, "Attach a file" put the file near the top, under the title, when you had not clicked into the text yet. In Raw mode it even went into the frontmatter. It goes to the end now.
- An image whose address Fluid cannot load (an unknown scheme, a `crystalline://` address) showed a broken image. It shows its alt text now.
- A Fluid tab left open across an upgrade broke without a word when it opened a screen or a part of one (a dialog, the Markdown view, the graph) it had not loaded yet. Now it says so. A screen offers Reload, a part offers Try again and then Reload.

## Changes

- Moving an engram to a new address, or moving a Reflection page, works now while somebody has it open in the editor. Before, the move was refused until the editor closed. The editor saves its text first (a text that cannot be saved is kept), then says where it went and offers "Open it there". Somebody who may not open the new place is only told that it moved. One limit on a shared Postgres database: each instance knows only its own editors. When an engram of a virtual domain is moved on one instance, an editor open on another instance is not told. Its next save finds the engram gone and pauses with a conflict.
- A page move onto a path that already holds a page (or its threads) now answers 409, not 422 (`POST /api/v1/domains/{domain}/reflection/move`). That is the same answer as a create over an existing page. A script that checks for 422 there needs an update.
- The REST API has `GET /api/v1/domains/{domain}/reflection/summary`, which answers what the Reflection home shows. The tree (`GET .../tree`) carries `folder_counts`, the engrams in each folder. A listing hit (`GET .../engrams` without a ranked search) carries `recorded_at`.
- An argument error for a domain served by another machine is now the same error a local call gives, not a tool failure.
- An agent reading an engram whose editor cannot save gets the saved version, with a note that somebody has unsaved text there.
- A wholesale replace (`write --overwrite`) of an engram open in an editor that cannot save is refused and names who is in there.
- The upload routes accept `If-None-Match: *`: a path that already holds a file answers 412. Fluid sends it for a new Reflection file, so an upload never replaces somebody else's file.
- Deleting or moving a domain's `MANIFEST.md` is refused (422). Edit it instead, or remove the domain.
- A domain name that differs from another domain's name only in letter case (`eng` and `Eng`) is refused when you add, connect or rename a domain, on every platform. A name taken from a folder or a MANIFEST steps aside instead. `crystalline doctor` warns about such a pair that exists already.
- Reflection pages that cite engrams read much faster on large domains.
- A Reflection read says who is editing the page right now, and the event stream announces Reflection changes.
- On a shared Postgres database, instances can start at the same moment, also on an empty database and after an upgrade. The guide no longer asks you to start one first.
- On a shared Postgres database, commands without a daemon (`sync`, `reindex`, `domain add`, `mcp --embedded`) act as this machine: they take the host lock of a free domain and give it back when they end. A domain another instance hosts is left out and named. `mcp --embedded` keeps its locks alive for the whole session. `crystalline status` suggests `crystalline sync --domain <name> --take-over` when a host went silent.
- `list_domains`, `crystalline status`, `doctor` and every refusal name a host by its label, not its id.
- Links in Fluid's sidebar, the avatar menu and the main pages start loading their screen when you point at them or focus them, so the next screen opens sooner.
- Fluid moved many controls and renamed some. Fluid has no public UI contract, but a script that clicks by name may need an update.
- The index is upgraded on the first start. An older Crystalline refuses the upgraded index.

## Breaking changes

None.
